About

Statically determinate 2-D pin-jointed trusses. Sign convention: tension positive. Determinacy check: m + r = 2j. The unit selector switches labels between SI (m, N) and US customary (ft, lb) — values are not converted, since member forces depend only on load magnitudes and geometry ratios. Enter numbers directly in your chosen system. Pins provide horizontal + vertical reactions; rollers provide one reaction along an adjustable direction (rotate in the Selection panel — 90° floor, 0°/180° wall). Compare results with Method of Sections, FEM, or measured bar forces from the GUNT SE 200.01.

Synthetic Dataset Generator

Systematically samples truss designs, solves each one, and exports machine-learning-ready CSVs: designs.csv (one row per design: all inputs + summary responses) and optional members.csv (one row per member, keyed by design_id, for member-level / graph models). All values are SI (m, N, mm, MPa, kg) regardless of the display unit setting. Sampling is seeded and reproducible.

Structural response

Physical test configuration

In the laboratory the finished truss rests on two supports a fixed distance apart, and the Instron compression plate is lowered onto it from above until the structure fails. This model represents that same arrangement. The predicted capacity is an analytical estimate built on the assumptions in Settings, and must be validated by building and testing the truss.

Everything here is live — change a value and the truss on the board updates immediately. Every setting is an assumption you can inspect and challenge.
Structure and fixture
Picking General, Bridge or Roof rebuilds the geometry immediately. Custom keeps whatever is on the board so you can build your own; any manual edit switches to Custom automatically.
Distance between supports, matching the laboratory fixture. Type any value from 10 mm to 50 m — 1200 mm is perfectly valid. Supports never move during optimization; use the Support tool to shift them within the span.
Joints sit on this grid. Type any even number from 4 upwards — 50 or more is fine, though a very fine grid makes the board slower to redraw and needs many members to stay determinate. Odd numbers are rounded up so mid-span stays on a grid line.
How tall the design space is. A deeper truss carries more load for the same member force but uses more material.
Gluing sticks face to face multiplies area by n and out-of-plane stiffness by n³ — and multiplies sticks consumed.
How many identical frames the specimen has side by side. Two planes share the load, so capacity doubles — but so does the stick count.
Member material (applies to new members and the Assign tool)
Every member stores its own material. Pick one here, then draw members or use the Assign tool to change existing ones. Editing any property below overrides that material everywhere it is used.
How members are joined. A glued lap fails in shear across the overlap; a lashing has a fixed capacity; an ideal pin imposes no joint limit at all (useful for seeing how much the joints are costing you).
Longest member you can cut from one piece of this material.
Broad dimension, lying in the plane of the truss. Sets area and the strong bending axis.
Thin dimension, out of plane. Area A = w × t; in-plane inertia I = t·w³/12; out-of-plane I = w·t³/12, about 25× smaller.
Used to work out the mass of the finished truss.
Stiffness along the grain. Buckling capacity is proportional to E, and deflection is inversely proportional to it.
Stress at which the wood tears when pulled.
Stress at which the wood crushes when squeezed. Governs short members; long ones buckle first.
String carries tension only. A string that ends up squeezed goes slack, and the model rejects the design.
Joints and buckling — the assumptions that matter most
How far two members lap at a glued joint. Longer laps give much stronger joints — the cheapest way to raise capacity.
Shear the glued interface takes before letting go. For PVA on wood the wood surface usually fails first, around 4–7 MPa.
Computed as width × overlap × shear strength for the selected material. Worth calibrating with a simple pull test — it directly sets what a tension member can carry.
Capacity of a lashed connection, used when a member's connection type is String lashing.
Important. A pulled member transfers force through the glue, so the joint limit applies. A squeezed member bears end-on and does not rely on glue shear, so normally it should not. Choosing "both" is conservative and was the single biggest reason earlier versions predicted very low capacities.
A lone flat truss buckles sideways using the small inertia. Two cross-braced planes restrain each other, so buckling happens in-plane using an inertia about 25× larger. Match how the specimen is really built.
Buckling capacity is π²EI/(KL)². K = 1.0 assumes freely rotating pinned ends; 0.5 assumes fully fixed. Used only when the box below is unticked.
When ticked, each member's K follows its connection: glued lap 0.85, string lashing 0.95, ideal pin 1.00. This is how connection type reaches the buckling calculation as well as the tension capacity.
Loading
The plate is lowered from above and bears on every joint at the top, sharing the load. A hook applies everything at one point, roughly doubling peak member force for the same total load.
The weight hung from the hook. This is the load you apply — set it yourself. With plate loading the ram instead ramps from zero to failure, which you drive with the Run load test control.
A flat plate only touches joints within this height of the highest one. A level top chord shares load between many joints; a peaked truss concentrates it on the apex.
What students are aiming for. 4448.22 N = 1000 lbf. Historical results in this laboratory have reached roughly 700 lbf (3114 N) and sometimes more.
Stick budget
Popsicle projects normally limit the number of sticks; competitions such as the ASCE balsa event limit total stock length. Both sets of numbers stay editable; the rule chosen here is the one enforced.
Lower limit so an optimized design cannot become trivially small. Set to 0 to remove the floor.
Total sticks available, counting every plane and lamination layer: members × lamination × planes.
For the total-length rule: how many full pieces may be cut up.
Length of one piece as supplied. 914.4 mm = 36 in.
String ties carry tension only, weigh almost nothing and do not consume the stick budget.
